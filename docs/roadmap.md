# Roadmap

Where the project stands and what comes next. Scores for each step are in [`experiments.md`](experiments.md).

## Done

- Setup: tooling, data on GCS, BigQuery external tables.
- Exploration of every table, summed up in [`exploration.md`](exploration.md).
- Baseline without trajectory (median fuel rate of the type × duration), 330 kg on val.
- Pipeline in `src/prc_fuel/` (load, split, clean, features, train, evaluate, run), so train, rank and final go through the same code.
- Scores on rank and on val without outliers. Most of the val error comes from a few flights with a wrong fuel rate, and keeping them in training actually helps (run #8, [outliers notebook](../notebooks/outliers_error.ipynb)).
- Route distance (great circle between the two airports, haversine). Available for every flight, even without trajectory.
- Trajectory features per interval, computed in BigQuery: number of points, altitudes, groundspeed, mach, and the distance between the first and last position. Impossible values are set to NaN, with thresholds taken from gaps in the data ([trajectory notebook](../notebooks/trajectory_features.ipynb)). The averaged `vertical_rate` didn't match the altitude change (correlation 0.42) and was dropped, the rate implied by the altitudes brought nothing either. Rank went from 248.6 to 241.2 (runs #10 to #13).
- OpenAP fuel estimate (fuel flow times duration, mass assumed from mtow to mlw over the flight). Spearman 0.93 with the fuel, but only because of duration and aircraft size, which the model already had: no gain (run #14, [OpenAP notebook](../notebooks/openap.ipynb)). Within a type the model already matches the real fuel rate better than OpenAP. A mass depending on route distance changed nothing either, all its inputs were already features.
- Time-based validation (`--split time`, last 20% of flights by takeoff in val). Tuning LightGBM on the random val made rank worse (run #15), because val flights have close flights in training. On the time split, val and rank order configs the same way (Spearman 0.94), so tuning is done there now: 127 leaves, at least 100 intervals per leaf, 75 trees at 0.05 (run #17, [tuning notebook](../notebooks/model_tuning.ipynb)).
- Mass from the climb ([climb mass notebook](../notebooks/climb_mass.ipynb)). About half the flights have a climb we can use (first point under 3,000 ft and no gap over 5 min before 25,000 ft). For those, the mass is the one that fits the climb best with OpenAP's thrust and drag. It comes out too light, 68 of the 894 A359 flights even end up stuck at the empty weight, and it's mostly noise: flights of the same route get anything between ~140 and ~210 t, and within a route it has no link with the fuel rate (Spearman -0.008). Probably because we only have groundspeed, so the wind is mixed in, and the thrust really used changes from flight to flight. Not added as a feature.
- Error on the intervals without trajectory ([notebook](../notebooks/no_trajectory_error.ipynb)). The ones under 0.5 points/s are a third of val and 69% of the squared error, but they hold ~3 times more fuel (751 kg on average against 261), and relative to that fuel they are the best predicted (0.34 against 0.42). The 0.6% with no altitude and no speed at all are badly predicted, but they only make 5% of the error, ~4 kg at best. Not worth filling them.
- Other models on the time split (runs #19 to #21, [tuning notebook](../notebooks/model_tuning.ipynb)). XGBoost with its defaults is worse everywhere (205.4 on val), probably overfitting, so I didn't tune it. Tuned CatBoost stays behind LightGBM on val (179.6) but does better on rank. Averaging the two beats both, 170.0 on val and 238.6 on rank against 175.8 and 243.5 for LightGBM alone, so the average became the reference (`--model blend`).
- Better cleaning, measured before doing anything. `mask_bad_values` only sets ~740 values to NaN on train, 654 of them boundary positions on intervals of a few seconds, so fixing them can't matter. What's really missing is the speed, absent on 35% of intervals. One speed column filled for ~96% of them (from mach, else groundspeed, else the distance between the bounds) changed nothing (run #22), the model probably already got it from distance and duration.
- Wind, tried on one week of ERA5 (1-8 August 2025, inside val_time), nothing committed. The tailwind at the middle of each interval has almost no link with the error of the blend (Spearman -0.06). Probably because airliners usually cruise at a fixed mach, so the wind changes the distance flown in an hour more than the fuel burnt in that hour. Dropped, also for the climb mass, which would still miss the real thrust.
- Fuel rate as the target (runs #23 to #25, `--target rate`). The model learns `fuel_kg / duration_s` and the prediction is multiplied back by the duration. CatBoost gets much better, 162.6 on val and 232.9 on rank against 170.0 and 238.6 for the blend, but LightGBM gets worse (184.9). Weighting intervals by their duration didn't help: CatBoost got worse, LightGBM a bit better on val but much worse on rank. Tuning CatBoost again on the rate found nothing better either, probably because early stopping watches the error on the rate and stops too early for a score in kg ([tuning notebook](../notebooks/model_tuning.ipynb)). CatBoost on the rate, with the settings tuned on kg, became the reference.
- Final score (run #26). CatBoost on the fuel rate, trained on all of train and fixed before looking at final: 233.2 on rank, 213.7 on final, 221.6 on both together.
- README rewritten with the results, the approach and what didn't work, plus a figure of the rank score over the runs (`scripts/plot_scores.py`).
